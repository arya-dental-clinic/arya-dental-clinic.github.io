Treatment information

Understand your options.

These overviews are general education, not a diagnosis. Select a topic to learn what it commonly involves; a dentist can advise whether it is appropriate for you.

◉

Root canal treatment

Care for a tooth with affected inner tissue.

What it is: Treatment that removes affected pulp tissue and cleans the inside of a tooth.

Why it may be needed: A dentist may discuss it when decay, injury or infection affects the pulp.

General procedure: Examination and imaging may be followed by cleaning, shaping and sealing the root canals. A restoration may be recommended.

Visits & after-care: Number of visits and recovery vary by case. Follow the dentist’s instructions and return for review as advised.

Consultation: Arrange an assessment for persistent tooth pain, sensitivity or swelling.

✳

Scaling & polishing

Professional removal of deposits from teeth.

What it is: Cleaning that removes plaque and hardened deposits from tooth surfaces.

Why it may be needed: Deposits that brushing cannot remove, gum concerns or a routine assessment may prompt a cleaning visit.

General procedure: The dentist or dental professional examines the mouth and cleans areas where deposits have collected; polishing may follow.

Visits & after-care: Sensitivity can occur temporarily. Brush gently and follow the advice given at your appointment.

Consultation: Ask about a cleaning if you notice bleeding gums, deposits or persistent bad breath.

＋

Dental fillings

Restore a tooth after removing decay.

What it is: A restorative material placed in a prepared area of a tooth.

Why it may be needed: A dentist may recommend a filling for a cavity or minor tooth damage.

General procedure: The tooth is examined, affected tissue is addressed and a suitable material is placed and shaped.

Visits & after-care: The dentist will explain eating, cleaning and any temporary sensitivity to expect.

Consultation: Seek an assessment for a visible cavity, chipped tooth or new sensitivity.

⌑

Crowns & bridges

Fixed restorations planned for individual needs.

What it is: A crown covers a tooth; a bridge replaces one or more missing teeth using supporting teeth or implants.

Why it may be needed: These options may be considered for a weakened, damaged or missing tooth.

General procedure: Examination and planning come first. Preparation, impressions or scans, fitting and adjustments may be involved.

Visits & after-care: Visits depend on the selected restoration. Keep the area clean and attend follow-ups.

Consultation: A dentist can compare a restoration with other options for your situation.

◇

Dentures

Removable options to replace missing teeth.

What it is: A removable appliance made to replace some or all missing teeth.

Why it may be needed: A dentist may discuss dentures when teeth are missing and replacement is desired.

General procedure: Assessment, impressions, design, fitting and adjustment are typical parts of planning.

Visits & after-care: Adaptation takes time. Clean and store the appliance as advised and return if it rubs or feels loose.

Consultation: Ask about options if missing teeth affect eating, speech or comfort.

⌁

Tooth extraction

Removal considered when a tooth cannot be retained.

What it is: A procedure to remove a tooth from its socket.

Why it may be needed: It may be considered for severe damage, certain infections or other clinical reasons.

General procedure: The dentist examines the tooth, discusses options and explains the procedure and expected care.

Visits & after-care: Recovery differs by person and procedure. Follow written after-care guidance and contact the clinic if concerned.

Consultation: Seek an assessment before deciding whether removal is needed.

⌗

Braces & aligners

Orthodontic options for tooth alignment.

What it is: Appliances that apply controlled forces to move teeth over time.

Why it may be needed: They may be considered for crowding, spacing or bite concerns.

General procedure: Assessment and planning determine suitability and the type of appliance.

Visits & after-care: Treatment needs regular monitoring and good oral hygiene; details vary from person to person.

Consultation: A dental assessment can help determine whether orthodontic care is appropriate.

♡

Children’s dentistry

Dental guidance and care for children.

What it is: Preventive guidance and dental care for children as their mouths develop.

Why it may be needed: Check-ups help review oral health, development and home-care routines.

General procedure: The dentist speaks with the parent or caregiver, examines as appropriate and explains any next steps.

Visits & after-care: Advice is tailored to the child’s age and needs. Keep brushing and follow-up routines recommended by the dentist.

Consultation: Ask a dentist about a child’s first visit or any oral-health concern.

✧

Dental implants

A fixed tooth-replacement option for selected cases.

What it is: A dental implant is a fixture placed in the jaw to support a replacement tooth.

Why it may be needed: It can be considered when replacing a missing tooth, subject to clinical suitability.

General procedure: Assessment, planning and staged treatment are involved; a restoration is attached when appropriate.

Visits & after-care: The process can involve multiple stages and healing. Maintenance and follow-up are important.

Consultation: A dentist can assess oral health and discuss alternatives.

☼

Gum care & whitening

Discuss gum health and cosmetic options.

What it is: Gum care addresses tissues supporting the teeth; whitening changes the appearance of natural tooth colour.

Why it may be needed: Bleeding or swollen gums merit assessment. Whitening may be explored for cosmetic reasons.

General procedure: The dentist checks oral health first and explains suitable options and limitations.

Visits & after-care: Advice depends on the procedure. Whitening can cause sensitivity and is not suitable for everyone.

Consultation: Have gum symptoms assessed; discuss whitening before using a product.

No online description can tell you which treatment you need. A clinical examination is required to recommend care.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions.

General information only. Contact the clinic or speak to a dentist for advice about your situation.

What happens during a dental consultation?

The dentist asks about your concern and relevant history, examines your mouth and may recommend further assessment. They can then discuss suitable options and next steps.

When is a root canal recommended?

A root canal may be considered when the inner tissue of a tooth is affected. Examination and, where appropriate, imaging help a dentist decide what care is suitable.

How often should teeth be professionally cleaned?

There is no single interval for everyone. Your oral health, gum condition and risk factors help your dentist suggest a schedule.

When should a child first visit a dentist?

Ask a dentist for guidance based on your child’s teeth and development. Early visits can help caregivers learn about brushing, diet and oral development.

What should I do if I have tooth pain?

Arrange a dental assessment to find the cause. Seek urgent medical help if you have facial swelling with trouble breathing or swallowing, or feel seriously unwell.

Are braces and aligners suitable for everyone?

Suitability depends on the teeth, bite and oral health. A dentist or orthodontic professional can assess your needs and discuss options.

What is the difference between a crown and a filling?

A filling repairs a prepared part of a tooth. A crown covers much or all of a tooth. Which restoration is appropriate depends on an examination.
